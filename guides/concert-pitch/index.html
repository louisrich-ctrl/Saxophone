Concert pitch and saxophone transposition

What key to play on your sax when the band or backing track is in a different key.

Saxophones are transposing instruments. When you play a written C on alto sax, the note you hear is an E♭. Other instruments such as piano and guitar usually play in concert pitch, which is the pitch you actually hear. So if a backing track is in the key of C, your sax has to play in a different key to match it.

  • Alto sax sounds a major sixth lower than written.
  • Soprano sax sounds a major second lower than written.
  • Tenor sax sounds a major ninth lower than written, which is an octave plus a major second.

Example: the band plays in G. Alto sax reads and plays in E. Tenor and soprano sax play in A. Some keys can also be written with sharps, for example G♭ is the same as F♯.

The fingering chart and scale pages use written pitch, so fingerings stay the same whichever sax you play. In the chart you can also switch between written and concert pitch in Scales and chords.
