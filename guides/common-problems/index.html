Common saxophone problems and fixes

Simple things to try first when something does not sound right.

Squeaks

Squeaks usually come from the reed or the embouchure. Check that the reed is centred and level with the tip of the mouthpiece, and that the ligature is tight enough to hold it. Take in about a centimetre or two of the mouthpiece, keep your bottom lip soft, and do not bite. A chipped or worn reed squeaks too, so try a fresh one.

Low notes are weak or crack

Low notes need every key covered fully. Check that your fingers are flat on the keys and not on the edges. Use slower, wider air and a relaxed mouth. If the low notes still will not speak, a pad may be leaking and a repairer can check it.

Notes crack between registers

Press the octave key just as you change notes, and keep your fingers close to the keys so they move together. Practise slowly, and use the chart to check the fingering for each note.

Too sharp or too flat

Biting the mouthpiece makes the pitch sharp, and a loose mouth makes it flat. If the whole instrument is out of tune, move the mouthpiece a little along the cork, in to go lower or out to go higher. The tuner shows you where you are.

The reed feels too hard or too soft

A reed that is too hard makes the sound stuffy and tiring to blow. One that is too soft sounds buzzy and thin. Beginners usually do best on a softer strength, often around 2 or 2.5, and a teacher can suggest the right one for you.

Looking after your reeds

  • Wet the reed before you play.
  • Wipe the reed and store it flat in a reed case.
  • Rotate between two or three reeds so none wears out too fast.
  • Replace any reed that is chipped, warped or dull.

If your instrument leaks or keys are bent, take it to a repairer. A teacher can also check your mouthpiece, reed and posture in person.
