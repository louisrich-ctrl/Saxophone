Breathing and long tones on saxophone

How to breathe, how to support the sound, and the simplest exercise for a better tone.

Breathe low and wide

Stand or sit tall with your shoulders relaxed. Breathe in through the corners of your mouth, and let your belly and lower ribs move outwards as the air comes in. Avoid lifting your shoulders. A big, relaxed breath gives you plenty of air without any tension.

Blow warm, steady air

Think of the air you would use to fog a mirror, not a puff to blow out a candle. The air should be warm, fast and steady. Your belly and lower ribs gently push the air out. You do not need to blow hard, because a good sound comes from steady air more than from strong air.

Low notes and high notes

For low notes, keep your throat and mouth open and relaxed, and use slower, wider air. Cover the keys fully, because a small leak makes low notes crack or go airy. For high notes, use faster air with a slightly firmer embouchure, but do not bite down on the mouthpiece. Biting makes the sound thin and sharp.

The long tone exercise

  1. Pick an easy note, like written B, A or G.
  2. Breathe in for four slow counts.
  3. Play the note at a medium volume for eight slow counts, keeping the sound and the pitch as steady as you can.
  4. Rest for a few breaths, then repeat. Next time try ten counts, then twelve.
  5. Move down or up one note at a time as the first notes get easier.

Try it with the tuner. The needle should stay still. If it wobbles, your air or your jaw is moving.

Good habits

  • Rest between exercises. If you feel dizzy, stop and breathe normally.
  • Practise a few minutes every day rather than one long session.
  • If anything hurts, stop and ask your teacher.

A teacher can hear and see what you are doing, so use this page alongside lessons.
