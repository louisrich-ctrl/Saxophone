How to use this chart, alternate fingerings and range

Saxophone fingering chart: alto, soprano and tenor

Tap any note to see its fingering and hear it. Choose alto, soprano or tenor, then normal notes, sharps or flats. The notes are written pitch, and the fingering is the same on all three saxophones.

Reading the diagram

A filled circle is a key you press and an open circle is one you leave up. Sharps are red and flats are blue. A sharp and its matching flat use the same fingering.

Written and concert pitch

Alto (E♭) sounds a major sixth lower than written. Soprano and tenor (B♭) sound a major second and a major ninth lower.

Scales and chords on the saxophone

Use the Scales and chords tool above the guide to see any major, minor, pentatonic, blues or chromatic scale, and common chord arpeggios, written out for alto, soprano or tenor. Tap a note to see its fingering, or press play to hear the scale or arpeggio. Key names can be shown as written pitch (your sheet music) or concert pitch.

Practice quiz

Test yourself with the quiz: it shows a fingering and you pick the note. It is a quick way to learn the notes by sight.

Alternate fingerings and trills

Some notes have more than one fingering. The alternates below are common for trills and fast passages, where a different key makes the move easier. Fingerings can vary between saxophone models, so check with your teacher.

  • F♯ (written F♯4): left hand 1-2-3, right hand 1 and Side F♯ key.
  • B♭ (written B♭4): left hand 1 and right hand 1; or left hand 1 and Bis key; or left hand 1 and right hand 2.
  • C (written C5): left hand 1 and Side C key.
  • F♯ (written F♯5): octave key, left hand 1-2-3, right hand 1 and Side F♯ key.
  • B♭ (written B♭5): octave key, left hand 1 and right hand 1; or octave key, left hand 1 and Bis key; or octave key, left hand 1 and right hand 2.
  • C (written C6): octave key, left hand 1 and Side C key.
  • F (written F6): octave key, left hand 2 and Front F key.

Range of each saxophone

This chart covers low B♭ to high F. Higher notes (altissimo) are possible but their fingerings vary a lot between players and instruments.
